Sound familiar?

You've studied your opening repertoire for hours. You understand the moves. But when the position appears on the board, you can't remember your preparation.

Even elite chess players forget their opening lines. Memory competitors, meanwhile, can memorise thousands of digits, playing cards and other information.

The difference isn't a photographic memory. It's technique.

The Chess Memory Palace shows you how to apply those techniques to your chess.

How does it work?

Build your own chess memory palace in five steps.

01

Draw the tree diagram

Map out the opening repertoire you want to memorise, including its branching variations.

02

Choose the setting

Choose a familiar place, such as a railway station, airport or neighbourhood.

03

Map the locations

Create paths through your setting that match the branches of your opening repertoire.

04

Select the picture words

Convert chess moves into memorable picture words using Image Notation.

05

Memorise the palace

Bring the images to life in vivid scenes, linking each pair of moves to a location.
